## C1. The tile is 272 pixels tall in code and "at most 216" in the chapter

`tiles-ports-wires.md` proposes a clamp of 96–216 px, and `INV-workbench.1`'s test asserts exactly that. `/Users/kentgang/git3/katalyst-wt/feat-03b-canvas/frontend/src/graph/geometry.ts` ships `TILE_HEIGHT = 272` — *fixed*, not clamped, with a comment arguing the opposite case ("a map of boxes that are all the same height reads as a layout"). Cheap now, a test failure later.

## C2. A supposed claim still gets a `Belief` of `1.0` that nothing is allowed to print

While a supposition holds, `World.beliefs[H]` must carry *something*, and the only legal something is `p = lo = hi = 1.0`. `Belief` permits it, `belief_out_of_range` does not catch it, and INV-7 is satisfied. Every surface is told to read `states` first and print the word — but the outline view, the delta rail and the summary sentence are three more readers, and one of them will print `1.0` eventually. **Say it once in `propagation.md`:** a claim whose state is `supposed` carries `1.0` so the path product has a factor to multiply, and no surface but the path product may read it. One sentence, and `beliefChip.test.tsx` already has the matching test.

## C3. "Agreement" means two different things, and one of them is about to be a column heading

FR-8 uses *agreement* for run-to-run disagreement across generations (stack 04). Record 0014 and `diff.md` use it for the share of the 2 000 versions that moved the same way. `STATUS.md` flagged this on 17 September and `diff.md` B3 flags it again — neither resolved it, and 03b is about to paint the word on screen as a rail column. The field name can stay `agreement`; field names are not on screen.

## C4. `Diff.summary` has no sentence for a diff in which nothing shifted

`summary` is a required `str` filled from a template whose first slot is "the top row of the rail". INV-multiverse.27 quietly dodges the empty case by quantifying only over pairs "with at least one row". A branch that moves no terminal — perfectly reachable with a `believe`, or a `retune` below the 0.005 floor — leaves the template unfillable. **Add the second template sentence:** *"&lt;edit&gt; moves no ending and leaves &lt;n&gt; claims untouched."* It is also the most honest answer that branch can get, and it needs no new field.

## C5. A claim's resolve-by day may not survive the 180-point series cap

A tile's headline is read on the claim's own resolve-by day; the series is sampled down to 180 evenly spaced points when the window is longer. ## How `diff` gets the paired per-version difference

`versions_of(world) -&gt; Versions` — a plain frozen record holding the day of each column, the per-version prior draws, the per-version likelihood per day, the per-version inner spread, the survival weights and which claims they apply to. It **recomputes** from the world's own three inputs rather than being carried on it: 2 000 × days × claims floats is millions of numbers and no browser should be sent them. Recomputation is exact, so a recomputed version *is* the version the world was built from. The cost is one extra engine run per world in a diff.

## The rule, in two sentences

Plates are placed in **one pass over the whole map, in the order the map lists its arrows**: each is offered spots along its own wire — the natural one first, then further along the same run, then stepped clear to one side and the other by the socket rule — and takes the first whose rectangle touches no tile, no tail mark and no plate already placed. And a clear band between two rows of tiles is no longer a single line: when two arrows want the same gap, the band is divided into **a lane each**, so their wires never coincide and neither can their readings.

## What changed

**New — `frontend/src/graph/wires/plates.ts`.** The pass. `socketsFor` derives where a wire leaves and arrives from the tile box alone (38% / 62% down the tile, 8 px off the edge — `Tile.tsx`'s own numbers, with a test pinning them to the real values); `plateSize` works a plate's box out from the words it will hold, the way `geometry.ts` works a tile's height out from its claim; `planPlates` walks the wires in map order and takes the first clear spot. Obstacles are the tiles (4 px margin), the tail marks, and the plates already placed (6 px margin). Nothing is measured off the page.

**`route.ts`.** `freeCorridor` became `freeBand` — it hands back the whole clear gap instead of its midline — and `planRoutes` now gathers which arrows want which band *before* giving any of them a line, then divides each shared band into lanes evenly, in map order. Keyed by the **band**, not by the strip: the strait and the strike cross different stretches of the map but want the same gap between the same two rows, and it is the gap that has to be shared out. `freeCorridor` is kept as the one-line form for its old callers.

**`CausalWire.tsx`.** Takes `data.plateAt` when the pass gave it one, and keeps its own placement as the fallback so it still draws standalone in a test. `MARK_STANDOFF` moved to `plates.ts` so the pass and the drawing read one number.

**The reveal wart.** A plate and a mark are drawn in `.react-flow__edgelabel-renderer`, a different layer from the wire's own `&lt;g&gt;`, so the rule in `canvas.css` that reveals the wires a column at a time could not reach them — the reading arrived a beat before the wire. **`Canvas.tsx` — the one file outside the wires, 11 lines.** The pass has to be handed every wire at once, so `mode`, `strength` and `lag` now travel with the routable list, `planPlates` is called beside `planRoutes`, and `plateAt` plus `wave` come back in the `data` the wire already carried.
